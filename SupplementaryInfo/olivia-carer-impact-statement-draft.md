## 1. The care this year has required

In the 12 months to 1 November 2026, Olivia attended **54 separate therapy
appointments** across three disciplines:

| Support | Appointment days | Provider |
|---|---|---|
| Physiotherapy | 25 | Centre of Movement (Caity Spence) |
| Occupational therapy | 17 | Centre of Movement OT |
| Speech therapy | 10 | Simone Seeney |
| GP / medical certificates | 2 | — |

She is also under **neurology**, **CHQ Disability Rehabilitation** (Children's
Health Queensland) and **ophthalmology** (Terrace Eye Centre), and attends
**after-school care one day a week** (17 days recorded this year).

That is an appointment roughly every week of the school year, not counting
hospital and specialist review.

## 2. When these appointments happen, and why that matters

Olivia's therapy is scheduled at **7:45am and 8:00am** — before school, because
that is when the appointments are available. Speech therapy has recently moved
from fortnightly to **weekly at 7:45am**.

A 7:45am appointment means the household is up, medicated, fed, dressed and out
of the house earlier than a school morning already demands, and that one of us
is unavailable for work at the start of the day. It happens most weeks of term.

**[needs your words]** What this does to the working day — hours lost, days
started late, work that has to be made up in the evening.

## 3. Medication

Olivia is prescribed **four concurrent anticonvulsant medications**:

| Medication | Dose | Schedule |
|---|---|---|
| Sodium Valproate (Epilim) | 10ml | morning and night |
| Keppra (Levetiracetam) | 5.2ml | morning and night |
| Topiramate | 75mg | morning and night |
| Perampanel | 4mg | nightly |

(Clobazam 5mg was previously prescribed and is recorded as ceased.)

Three of the four are twice daily. That is **two supervised medication rounds
every day of the year**, approximately 730 administrations, each of which has
to happen whoever is caring for her — at home, at school, at after-school care,
at a grandparent's house, on holiday.

Keeping that supply running required **44 separate refill or script actions**
in 12 months: roughly one pharmacy or prescribing task every 8 days.

**[needs your words]** What is involved in giving the medication — resistance,
timing around food, what happens if a dose is late or missed, who else has to
be trained to give it.

## 4. Supervision and support at school

Olivia's recorded NDIS goals (from the goals document of December 2024)
include:

- attending **speech therapy** to develop more advanced sentences and role

- attending **occupational therapy** to support **emotional regulation and
  concentration**;
- **teacher supervision with inclusion support** at school;
- physical goals around climbing and walking obstacles unassisted.

The reliance on an alternative communication device, inclusion support in the
classroom, and OT for emotional regulation all describe a child who cannot be
left to manage a school day unaided.

**[needs your words]** What supervision she needs at home and in public: what
she cannot be left alone to do, what happens without line-of-sight, how this
compares to her sister at the same age.

### Where these figures come from

- Appointment counts: distinct calendar dates per discipline for Olivia in the
  12 months to 1 November 2026. **Counted by date, not by calendar entry** —
  the same session often appears on two synced calendars, and counting entries
  would have overstated every figure.
- Medication list and doses: the medication records held for Olivia, active
  ones only.
- Refill count: distinct medication refill and script dates in the same period.
- Goals: quoted from the December 2024 NDIS goals correspondence. **If a newer
  plan or goals document exists, it should replace this** — two years is a long
  time in a six-year-old's development.
